Where to start

Three different readers want three different things.

I want to hear it →
The acoustic demonstration. A train approaching, passing and departing at the correct decibel difference against each receptor's measured background — then running continuously at the real headway.
Start with Brooklyn Bridge Park dog run, one pass-by, then switch to continuous at 20× speed.
I want the argument →
The repository README. Headline findings, the full document index, the method register with its honest status column, and a section titled “what has not been done” that is longer than most projects' results sections.
Read Three headline findings, then What has not been done.
I want the data →
The frequency and exposure dashboard. Every crossing by hour, route and direction; how many people are underneath; how long they stay; and three of this programme's own withdrawn claims, printed on the page.
Drag the coincidence window from 1 s to 29 s. The answer does not move. That is a finding about the feed, not the railway.

The five interactive artifacts

Each is a single self-contained HTML file. No build step, no server, no network access, no dependencies, no tracking. Download and double-click, or clone and open. Each applies the same provenance discipline to a different medium.

Agentic population model
Groups — not individuals — enter DUMBO at persona-specific gateways, follow scenario itineraries, contend for capacity, and accumulate a noise dose along their actual path. Deterministic, seeded, with a full event log.
The first panel: the same itinerary started ninety seconds apart gets a different dose. Then the rejected-propagation panel, which is a negative result and the most important thing on the page.
Frequency and exposure dashboard
Traversals by hour, route and direction across day, evening and night periods; pedestrian arrivals, departures, walkway flow and residents; and a four-cohort presence model that publishes its own non-identifiability.
The cohort card. The worker and visitor ranges overlap, and that overlap is the finding.
Acoustic demonstration
Web Audio synthesis pinned to the MTA measurements. Six receptors, single pass-by or continuous running at the measured headway, A-weighted meters, CEQR threshold marks and a running energy average.
Watch the running Leq settle onto 87.50 — then read why that convergence is a closed loop and not evidence.
Navigable 3D model
Both bridges, four zoom tiers from the whole crossing down to a single rail fastener, with anchored callouts, click-to-inspect components and a live scale bar that reads in inches at the finest tier.
Turn every provenance filter off. The viewport goes completely empty, and the display says so. That is the state of public knowledge.
Provenance-tagged section
The 2D cross-section of the Manhattan Bridge track zone, every component colour-coded and dash-coded by how well it is known, with the source rubric attached.
Turn off the DOCUMENTED filter and watch most of the drawing disappear.
A warning that applies to all five. The 3D model contains zero measured elements. The audio is synthesised, not recorded. The agent model's itineraries are invented. These are instruments for reasoning about a problem, not evidence about it, and each one says so in its own interface rather than in a footnote somewhere else.

What has actually been found

1. The problem is documented and then abandoned

The MTA measured the noise, in DUMBO, at named addresses, and published the levels. The levels are high. Nothing followed. The measurement exists and the response does not, and the gap between those two facts is where this programme lives.

2. Three instruments for recording city noise, and no rail category in any of them

NYC 311 has a complaint descriptor for ice cream trucks and one for “other animals”. It has none for trains. Neither does SONYC, NYU's 150-million-clip urban sound corpus, whose authors state in print that they built their taxonomy from the NYC noise code — which is where the absence originates.

Within 500 m of the park where the MTA measured 87.50 dB(A), residents filed 4,055 noise complaints since 2020 and not one of them can be about the train. That circle produced 117 complaints about ice cream trucks and 95 about barking dogs. The dogs at the dog run are complainable. The trains over it are not.

This is a mechanism rather than a motive, which makes it testable, and it yields the first remedy in the programme that a resident could act on this month: amending a taxonomy is a far smaller ask than solving the noise.

3. Exposure peaks in the early afternoon — and this programme got that wrong twice first

Exposure is people multiplied by events. Optimising on attendance alone pointed to the weekend afternoon. Optimising on train rate alone pointed to the weekday morning. Both were published here and both are withdrawn. Train rate is nearly flat from 07:00 to 19:00 while the number of people underneath changes by a factor of four, so the product peaks between the two: 14:00 on a weekday, 13:00 Saturday, 15:00 Sunday.

4. A population's composition cannot be recovered from its departure curve

A four-cohort model pins total non-resident presence to about ±10%. The split between workers and visitors inside that total swings by more than half again across thousands of parameter sets that fit the data equally well. A departure curve carries no job titles — someone present for eight hours looks identical whether they came to work or came for the day. On a Saturday the model is explicitly degenerate and says so in its own output.

5. Distance does not order the measurements

The DUMBO Archway sits directly underneath the structure and is the quietest of the four MTA sites. Brooklyn Bridge Park's Main Street section, several hundred metres away across open water, is the loudest — exceeding a line-source prediction fitted to the near sites by about 17 dB.

The design consequence is direct. Under-deck treatment — the intervention most often proposed, and the first item in the November 2025 residents' petition — would be applied where the measured problem is smallest. That is cheap to test: one afternoon, a meter and a tape measure.

6. And one model that was deleted before publication rather than after

Fitting a propagation model to the near sites gave agreement with ideal line-source spreading to within 0.15 dB, which looked like a real result. A sensitivity test killed it: jitter the eye-digitised positions by ±10 m and the fitted decay exponent ranges from 0.7 to 22.3 — from no decay at all to steeper than a point source. The data cannot constrain a propagation model and the agreement is coincidence. It is recorded on the agent-model page as a finding, because the near-miss is more instructive than the model would have been.

Data and code

Five Python scripts. No API key, no account, no payment method, and everything but the realtime poller uses only the standard library.

The generated data is committed alongside the scripts so that the artifacts can be audited without re-running anything: dashboard-data.json, pedestrian-data.json and cohort-data.json.

The most dangerous single fact in the data work. MTA hourly ridership counts entries — people leaving the corridor. Over a whole day entries and arrivals roughly balance, so a daily total looks correct. By hour they are close to opposite. Building an exposure curve on entries would have put the peak in the wrong place while looking more rigorous than anything preceding it.

How to read anything in here

This programme has failed adversarial review five times, always the same way: over-claiming from abstract-level reading. The conventions below exist because of those failures, not in anticipation of them.

  • Every quantitative claim carries a “locus” — the exact passage it rests on, quoted. If a claim has no locus it is an inference and is labelled as one.
  • Every source is rated 1–5 and marked VERIFIED SNIPPET UNVERIFIED. A 5/5 is a primary agency measurement read in full. A 1/5 is a search-engine snippet.
  • Errors are corrected in place, not deleted. The wrong claim is quoted as a blockquote and followed by the words “That is withdrawn”. The dashboard carries three of them. A repository that silently edits its mistakes is asking to be trusted about the ones it has not found yet.
  • Every document ends with “where this document is likely to be wrong.” Those sections are the best guide to how much weight any of it will bear.
  • Synthetic is labelled synthetic, on the artifact itself. Not in a footnote, not in a README somewhere else — in the interface, where someone screenshotting it will capture the caveat along with the picture.

What is missing, stated plainly

Nobody from this programme has stood in Brooklyn Bridge Park with an instrument. Thirty-two methods are specified. Two have been executed and one partially. Everything else here is a statement about documents and datasets.

The four cheapest things that would move this the furthest, in order:

  1. Method 21 — the taxonomy-blindness test. A database query against an API already used here, testing whether elevated-adjacent census tracts show different 311 complaint patterns. An afternoon. Free. Publishable whichever way it comes out.
  2. Method 31 — the decay transect. Walk outward from the bridge with a meter and establish where the affected zone ends. Nobody has ever done this, and that boundary sizes the denominator for every exposure figure in the repository. One afternoon.
  3. Method 28 — measure dwell time. A timed cordon count with a clicker. Presence is arrival rate multiplied by dwell; only the arrival rate has been measured, and the cohort model proved that no amount of further arithmetic will supply the rest.
  4. Q42 — is this preempted or merely unregulated? If elevated rapid transit is not federally preempted from local noise regulation, then the City could regulate it and has chosen not to — which inverts the entire advocacy strategy from seeking a federal waiver to amending a municipal form. One competent lawyer, one day. The highest-value open question in the programme.

How to help

In rough order of value. The full list is in the README's contributing section.

  • Post a recording. If you have ever recorded a train crossing the Manhattan Bridge from Brooklyn Bridge Park, DUMBO or the Williamsburg Bridge walkway, that recording is more useful to this programme than anything currently in it. The bar is far lower than people assume: spectral shape and event timing survive an uncalibrated phone, which is what FIELD-CAPTURE-PROTOCOL.md is about.
  • Falsify a “not found” claim. Five have already failed. The prior on others failing is not low.
  • Execute any method. Several cost an email, a form, or an afternoon.
  • Challenge the arithmetic. The event-duration derivation, the cohort identifiability result and the propagation rejection are all arithmetic, and each is either right or wrong. None has been reviewed by anyone. Three issues exist specifically to attack them: #26 (cohort model), #27 (agent model), #28 (propagation).
  • Reclassify a component's provenance. Anyone with structural knowledge of riveted lattice trusses will find misclassifications in the 3D model, and finding them is the point of publishing the classification.
One standing condition. The people who live under this bridge have been asking for help since 2008. Any contact with them must offer something before it asks for anything, must not represent this programme as more established than it is, and must make clear that its central artifacts are synthetic. Over-claiming to a community in that position would be a different and worse kind of error than the ones already made here.
